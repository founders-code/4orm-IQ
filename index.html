The experience, both sides

One file. The client fills it, you read it.

Nothing here is a form the client abandons halfway. Discovery is a conversation, and every answer they give lands in the same record you open.

01

They start on their own, before you have met

Intake asks who is deciding and what they are deciding. Nothing is scored yet and nothing is shared. If they stop here, you never see it.

Client
02

Discovery, in their words rather than yours

What they do, how long they have done it, what lands in the account, what is already on the mortgage. Then the one question nobody asks them: what would make this a good outcome. That answer is a blank box, never a menu, because a menu is us deciding what good looks like.

Client
03

What could go wrong, and what protects them

They score each risk on how likely it is and how badly it would land, then decide what to do about it and watch the score move. Every figure carries whether it was confirmed, stated, estimated, or not known yet.

Client
04

They choose what to share, and send you a link

4ormIQ never contacts you. The client hands over the link, and only then does the file appear on your side and ask for access. Identity and income come across as verified, not exposed. The full package stays sealed until they select a lender.

Client
05

You open a file that already explains itself

What they were asked, what they said, what they understood and what they signed, in sequence. When somebody asks two years from now why this was suitable, the answer is already written and dated rather than reconstructed.

What it changes for the business

Less time in discovery. More of the file already done.

Discovery arrives finished

The questions you ask in the first two meetings are answered before the first one, in the client's own words and with the date each answer arrived.

The record is a by-product

Nobody writes it afterwards. It is made as the decision is made, which is the only time it can be made honestly.

The client understood it

A record of what was put in front of them and what they did about it, rather than a box they ticked saying they understood.

Your process, not ours

Add a step, add a document, change the order. The control map tells you the moment a required control has been dropped out of your own process.

The AI your people already use

Connected and watched: what left the building, what a tool asserted, and which guardrails cannot be switched off by anyone.

The client keeps their copy

They hold the same record you do. Nothing in it is a surprise to them later, which is where most of the trouble starts.
