The evidence scaffolding

One check. One record. Three audiences read it.

A person about to send money, the business they are sending it to, and the regulator who may ask about it later all need different things from the same moment. 4orm builds that moment once, records it in a way that cannot be edited afterwards, and lets all three read it. This page is that structure, drawn.

Consumer

Am I about to make a mistake

Wants to know what the public record actually holds about the party in front of them, in words they can act on, before the money moves.

Company

What does the record say about us

Wants to see what was found, where it came from, and to correct it if it is wrong or out of date.

Regulator

Show me it happened that way

Wants proof a check ran, which rules were in force at the time, which registers were asked, and what was deleted when.

What it produces
    What it never does

    Where the intelligence is, and where it is deliberately not

    Three points on the map do the thinking. Everything between them is plumbing, and that separation is the design rather than an accident of it. The step that reads evidence has no authority to go and find more, so coverage is always counted from what was actually retrieved and can never be asserted by the step that would benefit from asserting it.

    One · The intake

    Conversational discovery

    A person describes what is happening in their own words and 4orm IQ works out what actually needs checking. It is built on motivational interviewing: open questions that start with what or how, never why, and roughly twice as many reflections as questions.

    That is the difference from a chatbot. A chatbot waits to be asked. This asks, because the person often does not yet know which of the four things in front of them is the one that matters.

    Two · The reading

    One reasoning pass over retrieved evidence

    Everything the registers returned is read once, and an assessment is formed with the provenance attached to every line of it. The step cannot search. If it wanted more evidence it would have to say so rather than go and get it, which is what keeps the coverage figure honest.

    Three · The pattern layer

    Signals no single register holds

    The same wallet under four company names. The same nameserver as a party that was acted against last month. A name being checked five hundred times today that no register has ever heard of. None of this exists in any one register. It exists between them, and only because 4orm keeps its own corpus.

    The same record, read three ways

    This is the interoperation. Nothing is generated a second time for a second audience, because a second generation is a second version, and two versions of the truth is the thing an audit is looking for.

    What is heldThe consumer seesThe company sees The regulator sees
    The ask Their own words, and the check that came out of them. That a check was run against them, and on what identifier. The input class only. The identifier itself is never stored in the clear.
    Which registers applied How many of them answered, and which did not. The same list, so they can see what was and was not asked. The applicable set, and the rulebook version that decided it.
    What came back The finding, with a link to the page it was read from. The same finding and the same link, with a route to correct it. The same finding, plus what could not be reached and why.
    The assessment Plain words, two numbers kept apart, no single score. The wording as published, so a correction has something to point at. The wording, the version, and the evidence it rests on.
    The proof it ran A reference number. The same reference number. The hashed row, and every row after it that depends on it.
    What was deleted Nothing to ask for. Almost nothing is kept. Nothing held about them beyond the corpus period. The deletion schedule, and evidence it ran on each day.

    Why the record cannot be edited afterwards

    Every run is a row, and every row carries the hash of the one before it. Change a row after the fact and every hash after it stops matching. That is what turns a number on a page into a number somebody outside the company can check, and it is the reason the same record can be handed to all three audiences without anybody having to take our word for the version they were given.

    The rule

    A run cites a version, never the rules themselves

    Which lets the rules change without disturbing a hash already written. The cost is that the version has to be recorded separately, so the rule history is chained too.

    The rule

    Silence is never clearance

    No register in the catalogue is allowed to have its silence read as a clean result. A register that could not be reached is reported as a register that could not be reached, and the run is marked incomplete.

    The rule

    Two numbers, never combined

    How confident we are that we found the right party, and how much of the applicable evidence we actually reached. They answer different questions, so they are shown apart. There is no single trust score and there will not be one.
