What they do with it. Your bank or credit union decides your claim, and if the money has not been taken out at the other end yet, they may be able to pull it back. This is the only thing on this page that can get money returned to you, and the chance drops every hour.

Timing. No bank publishes a deadline. Every one of them says to call immediately. Make this call before you finish reading this page.

What only you can answer

Your bank or credit union will ask you for these. Writing them down before you make contact is what turns a long call into a short one.

  1. Amount and currency sent
  2. Date and exact time of each payment
  3. How you paid: wire, e-Transfer, card, bill payment, crypto purchase
  4. Name on the receiving account, exactly as you typed it
  5. Receiving institution and country
  6. The account or reference number the payment went to
  7. Whether any further payment is scheduled or promised
  8. Whether you gave them remote access to any device
  9. Whether you gave them any banking credential, code or one time passcode

What to send with it

  • Your payment confirmation or wire receipt
  • The screen or message that told you where to send the money
  • This report

Before you send this

Say the words fraud claim, not disputed purchase. They are handled by different teams. Ask for your claim number before you hang up and write it down.

The Canadian Bankers Association says to contact your bank straight away and to keep every message, email and screenshot connected to what happened.

TD says that going to the police first will not make your bank claim any faster. Do both, in whichever order suits you, but do not wait for a police file number before you call the bank.
