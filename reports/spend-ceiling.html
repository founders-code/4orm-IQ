4orm IQSecurity hardening, step 2 CR-002 · v1.020 September 2026

Nothing is allowed to spend money before something checks it can

A spend ceiling that runs before the first vendor call, a ledger that records what was reserved and refused, and a reader in the control room that says what a check actually costs and what that figure is built on.

01

What was wrong

The route that spends money had a rate limit of five requests a minute, held in a map in memory, per instance, reset on every cold start. Its own comment said so: move to Vercel KV before this URL is public. It counted requests. It did not count dollars, and nothing anywhere counted dollars.

Two consequences followed from that, and neither was visible from the board.

A request cost whatever it cost Five requests a minute is a ceiling on volume. With a search clamp of 34 and a reasoning call of 16,000 output tokens, five requests a minute is not a ceiling on spend, and the two were being treated as the same number.
The documented ceiling was wrong .env.example said the search clamp was 3 to 14 with a default of 10. The code clamped 3 to 34 and defaulted to 22. Anyone sizing the bill off the file was out by more than half.
02

What was built

api/_budget.js One module. reserve() decides before the first vendor call and records the reservation as it decides, so a burst of concurrent requests cannot all read the same low count and pass. settle() writes what the run actually cost against what was reserved.
db/spend.sql The ledger. Salted client key, timestamp, estimate, measurement. No address, no identifier, no party. Eight day purge. Not yet run against the database.
api/admin-metrics.js A spend block on the existing route. No second door, no second auth path.
admin.html A spend pill on the reader row, and a reader behind it.
tools/budgetcheck.mjs
tools/spendcheck.mjs		 Twenty-nine assertions between them. Both in npm test.

Three counters, not one

Runs per window, dollars per window, dollars per day, all per client, plus a house ceiling for the day across everyone. Any one of them refuses.

The house ceiling never escalates. A reader who arrives on the day it is reached has done nothing wrong, and treating them as though they had is how a product punishes somebody for its own budget.

Volume throttles. Repeat behaviour blocks.

A client refused three times inside fifteen minutes is paused, and the pause doubles each time it happens again, to a six hour cap. One oversized request is refused and never paused, because size is not behaviour. There is a test that fails if that inverts.

It never falls open. Where Postgres is unconfigured or fails, the in-memory ledger carries the same rules on a single instance and the refusal still fires. Storage must never be able to break a check, and must never be able to let one through either.

03

What a check costs

A typical party runs 18 Exa searches, 11 of them asking for full text, and 4 Parallel objectives. At the published list rates that is $0.212.

Claudeper million tokensnot measured Default rate

Exa returns its own billed figure on every call and that figure is stored, so retrieval is an invoice number rather than a rate applied to a count. The reasoning half is not. No live run has had its token counts read back, and the rates in force are defaults I set rather than figures read off the invoice.

Until KBYS_RATE_CLAUDE_IN_M and KBYS_RATE_CLAUDE_OUT_M are set on the deployment, every dollar in the control room carries a line saying it is priced from a default. The screen is not allowed to let an estimate wear the clothes of a measurement.

The reservation is the ceiling of a check, not its average: 28 searches, 4 objectives, 90,000 tokens in and 16,000 out, which is $0.74. The average is what has to be affordable in aggregate; the ceiling is what has to be affordable when one request goes long.

04

The spend reader

A third pill sits on the operations row beside New and the fix list, carrying today's spend. It is never red for spending money. It warms as the day's house ceiling fills, and only the last band raises its voice, because that is the only band where anything is about to stop.

BandWhat it carries
The rate card What the dollars below are priced from. First, because everything under it depends on it being true.
Today Spent, left before checking stops, the house ceiling, tokens in and out, and a bar for the day.
What one check costs Average, median, ninth in ten, dearest. Tokens in and out per check. Reasoning against retrieval as a split. An average on its own hides the run that cost four times the rest.
The last 30 days Total, the two halves, average wait, and the last ten days one row each.
The ceiling Every limit in force, how many checks were refused today and why, and how many clients are paused now.

Where a run recorded no billed figure from Exa it is counted at nought for retrieval and the count of such runs is printed, rather than blending an estimate into a measurement. Where no run has been recorded at all, the reader says there is nothing to average and refuses to produce one.

05

How it was proved

Every fix has a test that fails if the fix is reverted. Each was reverted and the failure observed.

RevertedWhat failed
The refusal on a failed reservation budgetcheck, 2 lines
The gate moved to after retrieval budgetcheck, all 4 vendor calls
The spend pill removed spendcheck, 1 line
The rate card warning removed spendcheck, 1 line
The spend block no longer served spendcheck, 1 line

The ordering test is guarded against passing vacuously: if it can no longer find at least three vendor calls in the route to order against, it fails rather than reporting success on an empty list.
